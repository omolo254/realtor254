# Realtor254 Diaspora Homes: Business Plan

*A retirement venture helping Kenyans abroad buy high-end property in Kenya, safely.*
Prepared October 2026. Working draft, to be revised after the Phase 0 legal opinion and customer interviews.

---

## 1. Starting from first principles

### 1.1 What problem actually exists?

Strip the idea down to its bones: *a Kenyan abroad, with money, wants a good property at home and cannot be physically present to buy it.*

| What the buyer has | What the buyer lacks |
|---|---|
| Capital (savings, a foreign-income mortgage, sale of a property abroad) | Presence: they cannot walk the plot, sit in Ardhi House or meet the seller |
| Motivation: retirement home, family home, rental income, status | Time-zone overlap with Nairobi office hours |
| Networks (family, friends) | **People they can trust who are not conflicted.** Family members asked to "check on" a deal are a known fraud vector |

Listings are **not** the scarce thing. Agencies (Hass Consult, Knight Frank, Pam Golding, Villa Care and others) and portals (BuyRentKenya, Property24) already publish thousands. The scarce thing is **trust at a distance**:

- Diaspora buyers are disproportionately targeted by land and off-plan fraud: forged title numbers, developers who disappear after deposits are paid, land found registered to a third party at transfer (Nation, The Standard, Dawan reporting, 2024–2026).
- The Ministry of Lands has estimated that over 10% of title deeds in circulation may be fraudulent.

**So the product is not "property introductions". It is an independent, accountable, process-driven buying service: verify → transact safely → hand over → look after.** The introduction fee is how part of it gets paid for, not what is being sold.

### 1.2 Who is the customer?

Primary: **Kenyan citizens (including dual citizens) living abroad**, aged 35–65, buying KES 30M–250M (≈ US$230k–1.9M) homes in Karen, Runda, Lavington, Kileleshwa, Kitisuru, Ridgeways, Tatu City, Riverside apartments, or coastal homes in Diani, Kilifi and Watamu. Top source markets: the USA, UK, Gulf states, Canada, Germany and Australia.

Secondary: their non-Kenyan spouses (note: non-citizens may hold land only on leasehold up to 99 years, under Article 65 of the Constitution), and Kenyan professionals relocating home.

Jobs to be done:
1. "Tell me whether this property and this seller are real."
2. "Make sure my money only moves when it is safe to move."
3. "Be my eyes and ears, and give me a straight answer."
4. "After I buy, keep it occupied, maintained and earning."

### 1.3 The legal constraints and how they shape the model

These are the facts that most change the original idea. **Each needs confirming in a written opinion from a Kenyan property advocate before launch (Phase 0).**

| Constraint | Source | Consequence for us |
|---|---|---|
| Only persons registered with the **Estate Agents Registration Board (EARB)** and holding a current practising certificate may practise estate agency (negotiating, buying or selling land and buildings for others). Registration requires Kenyan citizenship, KCSE, ≥ 2 years' experience and a recommendation from a registered agent. | Estate Agents Act, Cap 533; EARB public notice, March 2025 | You cannot be the agent on day one, and **an informal "cut" from an agency is not recoverable in court**: *Ocra Realtors v Komen [2019] KEELC 1497* treated an unregistered agent's contract as illegal. → Work **under a written affiliate/business-introducer agreement with a licensed agency**, which stays the agent of record and does all negotiation. |
| Advocates may not share profit costs with non-advocates; paying someone to procure work for an advocate is an offence (touting). | Advocates Act, Cap 16; Advocates (Practice) Rules | **No referral cut from lawyers, ever.** Lawyer introductions are free. Offer a panel of at least 3 vetted conveyancing firms; the buyer chooses and pays the advocate directly. Turn this into a selling point: "Your lawyer works for you, not for us." |
| Real-estate agents are **reporting institutions** under POCAMLA (2023 amendments): customer due diligence on every buyer and seller, record-keeping, suspicious transaction reports to the Financial Reporting Centre. | POCAMLA ss. 44–48; FRC notices 2023 | Run KYC and source-of-funds checks from day one (passport, proof of address, bank statements), keep records for 7 years, and align with the partner agency's compliance officer. This also protects you personally. |
| Stamp duty is **4%** in gazetted towns and municipalities (including Nairobi) and **2%** elsewhere, based on the government valuation or the price, whichever is higher, paid through Ardhisasa / Ardhipay. | Stamp Duty Act; Ministry of Lands directive, April 2024 | Built into the cost calculator. Budget total closing costs of about 6–8% of price. |
| Your **country of residence** may regulate advising on, or marketing, overseas property, or require tax registration of the fee income. | Local law (US state, UK, etc.) | Add it to the legal checklist. Usually low risk for referral and advisory services, but confirm with a local accountant. |

**Design principle that follows: we never hold client money.** All deposits and purchase money go to the buyer's advocate's client account or a bank escrow. This removes the biggest fraud vector and the biggest regulatory and insurance risk at the same time.

## 2. Business model (hybrid)

### 2.1 Revenue streams

1. **Buyer concierge fee** (paid by the buyer, invoiced from your company):
   | Tier | Price (indicative) | What's included |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Verify** | US$1,500 per property | Independent title search via Ardhisasa (run by the buyer's advocate), site visit with geo-tagged video, beacon and survey check, neighbourhood and rates report, red-flag summary |
   | **Full Concierge** | US$4,000 or 1% of price, capped at US$12,000 | Verify for up to 3 shortlisted homes, coordination with agency, advocate, valuer and bank, weekly time-zone-friendly calls, document tracker, completion and handover |
   | **Aftercare** | 8–10% of rent collected, or a flat monthly fee | Tenant management, maintenance, rates and land rent payments, annual inspection (delivered through a licensed property manager) |
   *Wording matters:* the fee is for **coordination, verification and project management**, not for negotiating the sale, which stays with the licensed agent. Have the advocate approve the engagement letter.

2. **Affiliate / business-introducer fee from licensed agencies** (paid by the agency under a written agreement):
   - Kenyan sales commission is negotiable and commonly around 3% of price for residential sales (confirm with each agency).
   - A typical introducer share is 20–30% of the agency's commission (negotiate; get it in writing; payable on completion only).

3. **Developer introducer fees** for off-plan projects. Higher margins but the **highest conflict and fraud risk**. Only for developers that pass the off-plan checklist (approvals, NCA registration, escrowed deposits, track record). Phase 2 at the earliest.

**Never:** fees from advocates, a markup on valuations, or holding deposits.

### 2.2 Conflict-of-interest policy (published on the website)

- Every fee you receive is disclosed to the buyer in writing **before** they commit.
- You introduce buyers to **multiple agencies**, and can advise "do not buy" without losing your fee, because the Verify fee is earned whether or not the deal closes.
- If a buyer finds the property themselves, the Verify and Concierge services still apply.

### 2.3 Unit economics (worked example)

A KES 80M (≈ US$620k at KES 129/US$) home in Karen:

| Line | Amount (KES) | Amount (US$) |
|---|---|---|
| Agency commission at 3% | 2,400,000 | ≈ 18,600 |
| Our affiliate share at 25% | **600,000** | **≈ 4,650** |
| Full Concierge fee at 1% (capped at US$12k) | **800,000** | **≈ 6,200** |
| **Gross revenue per deal** | **1,400,000** | **≈ 10,850** |
| Less on-ground coordinator per deal (≈ 15%) | (210,000) | (≈ 1,630) |
| Less travel, searches, video, marketing allocation | (150,000) | (≈ 1,160) |
| **Contribution per deal** | **≈ 1,040,000** | **≈ 8,060** |

Buyer's own costs on the same deal: stamp duty at 4% (KES 3.2M), legal fees about 1–1.5% (KES 0.8–1.2M), valuation (≈ KES 100–200k), plus searches, registration and consent fees.

**Annual targets.**

| Scenario | Closed deals | Verify-only jobs | Gross (US$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (Year 1) | 4 | 6 | ≈ 52,000 |
| Base (Year 2) | 8 | 12 | ≈ 105,000 |
| Stretch (Year 3, plus aftercare) | 12 | 20 | ≈ 190,000 including ~US$30k recurring aftercare |

Fixed costs: website and CRM (≈ US$100/month), professional indemnity insurance (get quotes), company registration and accounting, coordinator retainer (≈ KES 40–60k/month), and marketing (webinars, diaspora events). Expect break-even at about 2–3 deals a year.

## 3. Operating model

### 3.1 The buyer journey (also the website's "How it works")

1. **Discover** (video call): goals, budget, financing, timeline, citizenship status, KYC.
2. **Shortlist**: curated homes from licensed partner agencies (or the buyer's own finds).
3. **Verify**: the advocate runs the official search; the coordinator visits; survey and beacons are checked; rates and land-rent clearance; for off-plan, approvals (NEMA, NCA, county) and escrow arrangements.
4. **Offer & negotiate**: done by the **licensed agent**, with us advising the buyer.
5. **Legal & funds**: the sale agreement goes through the buyer's advocate; the deposit (usually 10%) goes into the advocate's client account; Land Control Board consent if agricultural land; mortgage valuation; stamp duty through Ardhipay.
6. **Completion & handover**: transfer registered, keys, inventory video, utilities moved.
7. **Aftercare**: letting, maintenance, statements.

### 3.2 Remote-first tooling

- The buyer signs documents through their advocate; a **specific power of attorney** (not a general one) is used only where needed, executed before a notary public abroad and stamped and registered in Kenya.
- Video walkthroughs with time-stamped GPS, drone footage for land.
- Deal tracker (shown in the website's client portal mockup), WhatsApp Business plus email, calendar booking in the buyer's time zone.

### 3.3 Team

- **You:** founder, client relationships in the diaspora, sales, quality control. Your diaspora network is the main asset.
- **On-ground coordinator (Nairobi):** retainer plus per-deal bonus. Ideally a junior EARB-registered agent or someone working toward registration, which creates a path to your own agency later.
- **Panels:** 3+ conveyancing advocates, 2 valuers, 1 surveyor, 1 property manager, 3–5 lenders with diaspora mortgage products (KCB, Equity, Stanbic, NCBA, HFC: confirm current products).
- **Partner agencies:** 2–3 to start. Approach Hass Consult, Knight Frank Kenya, Pam Golding Kenya and Villa Care.

## 4. Go-to-market

- **Positioning:** "The safest way to buy a home in Kenya from abroad."
- **Content-led trust:** free *Diaspora Buyer's Guide* (PDF + web), a "10 red flags" checklist, a cost calculator, and monthly webinars at diaspora-friendly times (Saturday 10:00 EST / 15:00 GMT / 18:00 EAT).
- **Channels:** Kenyan diaspora associations (KDA-UK, Kenyan communities in Houston, Dallas, Atlanta, Minneapolis, Boston), churches, alumni groups (Alliance, Kenya High, Starehe, UoN), LinkedIn, Facebook groups, YouTube walkthroughs, and Kenya diaspora investment expos.
- **Referral loop:** a referral thank-you to past clients, structured legally (a gift or credit, not a split of agency commission).
- **Proof:** case studies. The first 3 clients get a founding-client discount in exchange for a written testimonial.

## 7. Confidence review (iterated)

Each claim the plan depends on was challenged. *Status key:* ✅ verified against a public source · ⚖️ needs a Kenyan legal opinion · 🔎 assumption to test in Phase 0.

**Iteration 1, original idea: "get a cut from Hass Consult and the lawyers".**
- ❌ A cut from lawyers is prohibited (Advocates Act fee-sharing and touting rules). *Changed:* lawyer introductions are free and panel-based.
- ❌ An informal cut from an agency is unenforceable for an unregistered person (Cap 533; Ocra Realtors v Komen). *Changed:* written affiliate agreement, with the agency as agent of record.
- ❌ Depending on one agency puts the business at risk. *Changed:* multi-agency, plus a buyer-paid fee.

**Iteration 2, hybrid model.**
- ⚠️ Being paid by both sides creates conflict. *Changed:* disclosure policy and a fixed Verify fee earned regardless of outcome.
- ⚠️ Handling client money creates fraud and regulatory exposure. *Changed:* never hold client money.
- ⚠️ AML obligations were not in the plan. *Changed:* KYC and source-of-funds process added.

**Iteration 3, economics and market.**
- 🔎 3% commission and a 20–30% introducer split are typical-market assumptions → confirm in agency calls.
- 🔎 Willingness to pay US$1.5k–4k → test in interviews.
- ✅ 4% / 2% stamp duty (EY tax alert, April 2024).
- ✅ Prime suburb land values are rising (Hass Consult Q4 2025 index: Karen KES 76M/acre, Runda KES 101.1M/acre).

**Remaining open high-severity item:** Risk #1 (legal structure). It has a named next step and owner (Phase 0 advocate consultation). Nothing else high-severity is unmitigated.

**Confidence:** High that the *trust-first concierge + licensed-agency affiliate* model is the right shape, and that the revenue math works at modest volume. Medium on the exact fee levels and split percentages until the Phase 0 calls and interviews. **Do not launch paid services before the legal opinion.**

## Appendix A: customer interview script (20 minutes)
1. Have you bought, or tried to buy, property in Kenya from abroad? What happened?
2. Who did you rely on? What went well or badly?
3. What would stop you buying in the next 2 years?
4. If an independent service verified the title, visited the site and coordinated everything, what would it be worth to you? (Let them anchor first, then test US$1,500 and US$4,000.)
5. How would you want to receive updates (WhatsApp, email, video)? How often?
6. Which areas and price range interest you? Cash or mortgage?
